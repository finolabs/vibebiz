# Promotion patterns

Patterns to recognize in an idea, and the cost each one hides. Use them to ask what the promotion is meant to achieve and what it really costs.

## Clearance at season end
Cut prices on leftover stock to free cash and shelf space for the next season. Hidden costs: low margin or a loss; customers learn to wait for the end of the season; leftover sizes are the hardest to sell. Ask: what share of the season's stock is cleared this way, and at what average margin?

## Markdown erosion of margin
Small cuts repeated across the season add up: a style cut three times may sell below cost. Often nobody sees the total because each cut is approved alone. Ask: who approves a cut, and does anyone see the combined cut per style?

## Bundles and "buy more, save more"
Combos (top plus bottom, buy 2 get a discount) lift units per transaction and move slow items alongside fast ones. Hidden costs: discount given to customers who would have bought both anyway; a slow item can drag the whole bundle. Ask: what did customers buy before the bundle, and what is the margin of the bundle?

## Seasonal events
Lunar New Year, back to school, mid-season and year-end sales, and marketplace shopping days (double-digit dates). Large volume, strong competition, heavy logistics load. Ask: how much of the year's revenue sits in these weeks, and can stock and staff cope?

## Vouchers and loyalty offers
Targeted offers to returning customers or to win back lapsed ones. Cheaper than broad discounts when well aimed. Hidden cost: giving the offer to people who were about to buy anyway. Ask: is there a comparison group that did not receive the offer?

## New-collection full-price window
Keep new styles at full price for a fixed time to protect margin and brand. Ask: how many weeks, and what happens to the stock that does not sell in that window?
