# Ansoff matrix

A two-by-two of growth routes. One axis asks whether the customers are the ones the business already serves or new ones; the other asks whether the products are the ones it already sells or new ones. The four cells run from the safest (sell more of what exists to who exists) to the riskiest (new products for new customers). It turns "we want to grow" into routes that can be compared side by side.

## When to use

- The idea is about growth (revenue, stores, customers) and the alternatives (D) hold one route, or routes that were named but never compared.
- People argue for different kinds of growth: a new region or channel, a new range, more sales to current customers.
- The board needs to see how much risk each route carries before it picks one.

## When not to use

- The choice is how to split money across product lines the business already sells, with no new market or product on the table: `bcg-matrix` fits better.
- The routes are ways to get one capability (make it, buy it, work with a partner): `build-buy-partner-roi` fits better.
- The outcome and its baseline are not set (C is not Clear): growth routes need a target to be compared against.

## Inputs

- The growth target from the outcome lens, with its baseline and period.
- The routes people have named, in their own words.
- What the business sells today and to whom (ranges, channels, regions), from the spec.
- Any result on file from a past move into a new region, channel or range.

## Questions

Ask about the routes people named and what the business already knows, one round at most.

1. "Ngoài hướng đã ghi, những hướng nào để tăng doanh thu đã được ai đề xuất? Ví dụ bán thêm cho khách hiện có, mở vùng hay kênh mới, thêm dòng hàng mới." (Offer the routes the files name.)
2. "Với từng hướng, mình đã có khách sẵn chưa, và đã có hàng sẵn chưa?" (This places each route in a cell; never ask the user to place it.)
3. "Công ty đã từng mở vùng, kênh hay dòng hàng mới nào chưa? Kết quả ra sao?"
4. "Nếu chỉ đủ sức đi một hướng trong năm tới, hướng nào nên đi trước, và vì sao?"

A market size, share or growth rate nobody present can give becomes a measurement plan or an open question (ask protocol rules 5 and 10), never an estimate of yours.

## Writes to

- Alternatives (D): each growth route beside "do nothing", with its cell (existing or new customers, existing or new products), what the business already knows about it, and its risk in plain words.
- Decisions: the route the user chose and why, as an `answer` entry when the user gave it.
- Diagram brief: a quadrant with existing and new customers on one axis, existing and new products on the other, and each named route placed in its cell.

## Common misuse

- Treating a new region with the same range as low risk; new customers carry real risk even with known products.
- Filling all four cells to look complete; place only the routes someone actually proposed.
- Ranking routes by market sizes nobody on file gave.
- Skipping "do nothing" and "sell more to current customers", the baseline every bolder route must beat.

## Fashion example (fictional)

The fictional office-wear chain "Sông Lam" (25 stores in the north) wants revenue up in 2027. Three routes were proposed:

| Route | Customers | Products | Cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loyalty push in the 25 stores | Existing | Existing | Sell more of what exists |
| Open stores in the central region | New | Existing | New market |
| Add a kidswear range for current shoppers | Existing | New | New product |

The chain has never opened outside the north and has no kidswear supplier, so both bolder routes are written as untested, and the loyalty push becomes the baseline the board compares them against.
