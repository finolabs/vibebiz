# North Star metric

Chooses the one number that shows customers getting the value a product or programme exists to give, and that grows when the business grows from it. Each team then sees its own number as an input to that one. It settles which number the whole company watches, before anyone breaks it into drivers.

## When to use

- The idea is a product or programme customers use again and again (a loyalty app, a styling service, a member programme), not a one-off change.
- Several teams each follow their own number (downloads, points earned, member revenue), and nobody can say whether customers get value.
- The board must decide whether to keep investing, and needs one number to judge by.

## When not to use

- The top result is already agreed (store revenue, return rate), and the question is which drivers make it up: use `driver-tree`.
- The idea is a one-off process fix with a pass or fail result; one number for value adds ceremony.
- The problem itself is still unclear: finish problem framing first.

## Inputs

- The value the product gives customers, in the user's words.
- The numbers each team reports today, with source and period.
- How often a customer would use the product when it works.

## Questions

One round at most. Offer the teams' current numbers and one value-based candidate as options.

1. "Khi ứng dụng (hoặc chương trình) này thực sự có ích, khách làm được điều gì mà trước đó không làm được?"
2. "Con số nào tăng chỉ khi khách nhận được điều đó, chứ không tăng chỉ vì mình quảng cáo nhiều hơn? Ví dụ: số thành viên mua lại trong 90 ngày (Đề xuất), lượt tải ứng dụng, điểm đã tích."
3. "Con số đó hiện là bao nhiêu, lấy từ báo cáo nào, của giai đoạn nào?" (a fact to look up first; without one, record a measurement plan)
4. "Các con số mỗi nhóm đang theo góp vào con số chung đó thế nào?" (the inputs)
5. "Con số nào không được xấu đi khi đẩy con số chung, ví dụ biên lợi nhuận hoặc chi phí điểm thưởng?"

## Writes to

- Outcome and measurement (C): the chosen number as the outcome statement's measure, its baseline or measurement plan, the three to five input numbers with the team that owns each, and the guardrail.
- Decisions: the chosen number and the candidates set aside, with why.
- Open questions: each input number without a source.
- Diagram brief: a tree with the chosen number at the top and the input numbers below, each labelled with its owning team.

## Common misuse

- Choosing a number that grows with spend alone (downloads, impressions); it shows activity, not value.
- Choosing revenue itself; it lags and hides whether customers got value.
- Keeping several "north stars", one per team; the point is a single number.
- Inventing a baseline or target. Record only figures the user or the files give, with source and period; a target is the user's decision.

## Fashion example (fictional)

The fictional chain "Sen Hồng" runs a loyalty app. Marketing counts downloads, customer care counts points earned, and stores count member revenue; all three rise. The one number becomes "members who buy again within 90 days", because it rises only when members come back. Its inputs are members who open an offer, members who visit a store after an offer, and members who use a size note saved in the app; the guardrail is the cost of points per order.
