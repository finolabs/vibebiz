# Kotter's eight steps

A way to lead a large change from the top, in order: make the need urgent, build a group of leaders who drive it, set one picture of the result, tell it widely, remove what blocks people, show early wins, keep pushing after them, and make the new way part of how the company runs. The steps are a timeline the plan follows.

## When to use

- The change reaches many teams or the whole chain (a move to selling across store and online channels, a new way of running every store), not one tool for one group.
- Organizational change (L) is Missing or Partial, and the plan has no named leaders, no early win and no way to keep the change going.
- Earlier company-wide changes faded after the launch or were seen as one director's project.

## When not to use

- The change is one tool or routine for one group: `adkar` fits better, since it plans one group's take-up step by step.
- The open question is why the parts of the company pull against each other (targets, structure, systems): `mckinsey-7s` fits better, since it finds the misalignment first.
- The idea is a small pilot whose result is not known yet; lead the change after the test, not before.

## Inputs

- Who sponsors the change and who else at the top must back it.
- Why now: the cost or risk of staying as is, from `## Problem & Why`.
- The teams affected and what they lose or gain.
- The pilot or first phase, as a source of an early win.

## Questions

1. "Vì sao việc này phải làm ngay bây giờ, và nói thế nào để cả chuỗi thấy điều đó?"
2. "Những ai trong ban lãnh đạo sẽ cùng đứng ra dẫn việc này, ngoài người khởi xướng?"
3. "Kết quả sau cùng là gì, nói trong một câu mà nhân viên cửa hàng cũng hiểu?"
4. "Điều gì đang cản nhân viên làm theo cách mới: chỉ tiêu, quy trình hay công cụ?"
5. "Trong ba tháng đầu, kết quả sớm nào có thể cho mọi người thấy?"
6. "Sau khi triển khai, điều gì giữ cho cách làm mới không trở lại như cũ?"

A step the user cannot answer becomes an open question; a leader the user only hopes will back the change is an assumption.

## Writes to

- Cost & Risk and Plan (L): the leaders and sponsor, the urgency message, blockers to remove, the early win tied to the pilot, and how the change is kept up.
- Pilot roadmap: the early win at the end of the first phase.
- Risks: leaders not yet on board, blockers no one owns.

## Common misuse

- Skipping urgency and starting with training.
- A leading group of one person.
- Declaring success at the first win and stopping the push.
- Using it for a change only one team feels; it adds weight the change does not need.

## Fashion example (fictional)

A fictional chain, "Gió Nam", moves all 60 stores to ship online orders from store stock. Earlier attempts faded because only the e-commerce director pushed. The plan names the sales and supply directors as co-leaders, uses the lost online orders from last Tet as the urgency, removes the store target rule that counted only walk-in sales, and sets as the early win ten pilot stores shipping same-day within six weeks. Store targets that count shipped orders keep the change going.
