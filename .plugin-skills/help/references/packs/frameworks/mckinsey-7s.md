# McKinsey 7S

A check of whether seven parts of the company line up behind a change: strategy, structure, systems, shared values, style of leadership, staff and skills. The parts that pull against the change are where the plan must act before rollout.

## When to use

- The idea needs teams to work in a way the company's targets, structure or systems still punish (stores rewarded only for their own sales when the idea sends their stock to other stores).
- Organizational change (L) is Missing or Partial, and the user suspects the change will fail for reasons beyond training.
- A similar change worked in one region and failed in another.

## When not to use

- The parts already line up and the open question is one group's take-up: `adkar` fits better.
- The misalignment is known and the plan needs leaders to drive the change across the chain: `kotter` fits better.
- The change touches one routine and no target, role or system.

## Inputs

- The goal of the idea, from `## Problem & Why` and `## Outcome`.
- How the teams involved are organised, measured and paid today.
- The systems they use and the skills the new way needs.

## Questions

1. "Chỉ tiêu và thưởng hiện nay của các bộ phận liên quan có khuyến khích cách làm mới không, hay ngược lại?"
2. "Ai báo cáo cho ai, và cách chia bộ phận có làm việc này bị kẹt giữa hai nơi không?"
3. "Hệ thống và quy trình đang dùng có cho phép làm theo cách mới không?"
4. "Nhân viên đã có kỹ năng cần thiết chưa, và người quản lý có thói quen nào cản việc này không?"
5. "Điều công ty coi trọng nhất trong cách làm hằng ngày có hợp với thay đổi này không?"

An answer nobody present can give becomes an open question; a guess about how a team behaves is an assumption to test in the pilot.

## Writes to

- Cost & Risk and Plan (L): each part that pulls against the change, the change needed in it, and its owner.
- Risks: misaligned targets or systems left as is.
- Open questions: parts the user could not describe.

## Common misuse

- Listing all seven parts with equal weight; the plan acts only on the ones that pull against the change.
- Describing the company as it should be, not as it is.
- Stopping at the diagnosis with no owner or change per misaligned part.

## Fashion example (fictional)

A fictional chain, "Đồi Thông", wants stores to hand slow sizes to nearby stores that sell them. Strategy and skills line up, but store managers are paid on their own store's sales and the stock system records a transfer as a loss for the sender. Those two parts pull against the change, so the plan credits the sale to both stores and fixes how transfers are recorded before the pilot.
