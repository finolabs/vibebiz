# Assumption map

## When to use
- The idea rests on beliefs nobody has checked ("khách sẽ dùng gợi ý size", "nhân viên sẽ nhập dữ liệu").
- You must pick what to test first with limited money and time.
- The user says "chắc chắn" about things that have no evidence on file.

## When not to use
- The idea is too vague to hold assumptions; clarify the problem and the first journey first.
- You need to imagine how a rollout fails: use `pre-mortem`.
- You already have a list of tested facts; ask the open gap instead.

## Inputs
- Assumptions already on file, plus beliefs stated in the user's answers.
- The solution and first journey in plain words.

## Questions
Sort assumptions by five kinds: customers want it, the money works, it can be built, people can use it, and nobody is harmed or breaks a rule. Then rank by how much the idea fails if it is wrong, and how little evidence exists.
1. "Để ý tưởng này thành công, những điều nào phải đúng?" (open question; also draw from earlier answers and quote them)
2. For each: "Anh/chị đã có bằng chứng gì rằng điều này đúng, ví dụ số liệu hay một lần thử?" Options: "Có số liệu", "Mới nghe nói", "Chưa có gì".
3. "Nếu điều này sai, ý tưởng còn đáng làm không?" Options: "Không còn (Đề xuất nếu hậu quả nặng)", "Còn nhưng phải đổi hướng", "Vẫn đáng làm".
4. Confirm the riskiest one: the most damaging if wrong and the least evidence. Ask: "Cách rẻ nhất để kiểm tra điều này trong hai tuần là gì?"

## Writes to
- Assumptions file: each belief with kind, evidence level and owner of the check.
- Cost and risk: the riskiest untested assumption, marked as such.
- The plan: the cheapest check, handed to `test-and-control-pilot` when it needs a pilot.
- A diagram brief (quadrant, importance against evidence).

## Common misuse
- Treating everything as an assumption and testing nothing.
- Counting a manager's opinion as evidence.
- Mapping only desirability and skipping viability and ethics.
- Choosing the easiest assumption to test instead of the riskiest.

## Fashion example (fictional)
Chuỗi Lụa Xanh plans size recommendations. Assumptions: customers will answer 3 questions about height and weight (desirability, no evidence); size data covers most items (feasibility, partial); staff will explain the tool (usability, none). The riskiest untested one is the first: if customers skip the questions, the tool has no input. Cheapest test: a printed QR code at 3 fitting rooms for two weeks, counting completions.
