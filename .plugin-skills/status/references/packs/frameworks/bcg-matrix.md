# BCG growth-share matrix

A two-by-two for a set of product lines the business already runs. One axis is how fast the line's market grows; the other is how strong the business is in that market compared with its biggest rival. Lines that are strong in a slow market fund the rest; lines that are strong in a fast market earn more money; lines that are weak in a fast market need a bet or an exit; lines that are weak in a slow market are candidates to shrink. It turns "where do we put next year's money" into a comparison across lines.

## When to use

- The idea splits a fixed budget (stock, marketing, store space) across product lines, brands or channels the business already sells.
- The alternatives (D) and the cost and return (M) are open because nobody has compared the lines with each other.
- People describe lines as "selling steadily but flat" or "growing fast but we are small there", which is the matrix's own language in plain words.

## When not to use

- The choice is a growth direction (a new region or channel, a new range, more sales to current customers): `ansoff` fits better.
- There is one product line, or the lines do not compete for the same money.
- Nobody on file can say even roughly whether each line's market grows or how the business compares with rivals; record the measurement plan first and come back.

## Inputs

- The product lines and their sales and margin on file, each with its source and period.
- What the user or the buying team knows about each line's market: growing, flat or shrinking, and from which report.
- The biggest rival in each line and how the business compares, in the user's words.
- The budget to split and its limits (the approval ceiling, lines that must stay).

## Questions

Ask about each line the business sells, one round at most. Market growth and share are the user's view with a source, or they become open questions.

1. "Trong các dòng hàng đang bán, dòng nào thị trường còn tăng, dòng nào đi ngang hoặc giảm? Anh/chị dựa vào báo cáo hay nguồn nào?"
2. "Ở từng dòng, so với đối thủ bán mạnh nhất, mình đang bán nhiều hơn, ngang bằng hay ít hơn?"
3. "Dòng nào đang mang về tiền đều đặn nhất, và dòng nào đang cần thêm tiền để lớn?"
4. "Có dòng nào bắt buộc phải giữ, dù kết quả thế nào không? Vì sao?"

Never invent a market growth rate, a market share or a margin. A figure nobody present can give becomes a measurement plan or an open question (ask protocol rules 5 and 10). Every money figure the plan derives from the split is an estimate labelled "not tech-reviewed".

## Writes to

- Alternatives (D): each line placed in its cell, with the source of the growth and strength views, and the routes per line (add money, hold, shrink).
- Cost & Risk and Plan (M): the proposed split of the budget in low, base and high, labelled as estimates and "not tech-reviewed", and the line whose result decides the next split.
- Open questions: each growth or share figure nobody could give, with who could measure it.
- Diagram brief: a quadrant with market growth on one axis and strength against the biggest rival on the other, one dot per line, with source and period.

## Common misuse

- Filling the axes with guessed growth rates or shares to make the chart look exact.
- Reading "weak in a slow market" as "close it now"; such a line may still bring in traffic or serve loyal buyers.
- Mixing lines with new ideas; a new range has no position yet and belongs to a growth-route comparison.
- Splitting the budget by the chart alone, without the margin and the limits on file.

## Fashion example (fictional)

The fictional office-wear chain "Sông Lam" splits a flat 2027 stock budget across four lines. The buying team's views, each tied to the 2026 sales report:

| Line | Market | Against the biggest rival | Cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shirts | Flat | Stronger | Funds the rest |
| Trousers | Flat | Weaker | Candidate to shrink |
| Office dresses | Growing | Weaker | Bet or exit |
| Coats | Growing | About equal | Not yet clear |

Nobody could give the dress market's growth rate, so it is an open question for the buying team, and the plan's split stays a range labelled "not tech-reviewed".
