# Jobs to be done

## When to use
- The user describes a solution or a feature and the real need of the customer or the staff member is unclear.
- You need to know who the idea serves, in which situation, and what they would otherwise do.
- Both shoppers and store staff are affected and may have different goals.

## When not to use
- The problem is a measurable breakdown with a cause to find: use `five-whys` or `fishbone`.
- The idea is purely internal reporting with one known user and a clear need.
- The user cannot describe any real customer or staff situation yet.

## Inputs
- A real recent situation: who, where, when and what triggered it.
- What the person does today instead (workaround, competitor, doing nothing).

## Questions
Describe the "job" as: in this situation, the person wants to get this done, so that this follows.
1. "Lần gần nhất có khách hoặc nhân viên gặp khó ở việc này là khi nào, tình huống cụ thể ra sao?" (open question)
2. "Lúc đó họ đang cố hoàn thành việc gì?" Offer two to four jobs drawn from the user's story, for example "Chọn đúng size mà không phải thử nhiều (Đề xuất)", "Mua quà kịp trước ngày tặng".
3. "Hiện giờ họ xoay xở thế nào?" This gives the alternatives and the real competitor.
4. "Điều gì khiến cách hiện tại chưa ổn: tốn thời gian, tốn tiền, hay rủi ro mua sai?"
5. Ask who else is affected: staff, store manager, back office.

## Writes to
- The problem section: the job statement for each user group.
- The users and stakeholders part of the spec: who benefits, who is affected, who may resist.
- Assumptions, for needs the user infers but has not heard from the person.

## Common misuse
- Describing people by age or income instead of the situation they are in.
- Writing a job that already contains the solution ("muốn một ứng dụng đặt hàng").
- Treating the user's own wish as the customer's need without a real case.
- Forgetting staff, who often carry the work the idea changes.

## Fashion example (fictional)
Chuỗi Lụa Xanh wants an app for shoppers to scan items. Asking for the last real case: a shopper buying a work dress for Monday waits 15 minutes for a fitting room on Saturday. Her job: leave with a dress that fits, in under 30 minutes, without trying five sizes. Staff job: keep the floor tidy while answering size questions. The need is faster fit decisions, so the options become size guidance and fitting-room queue handling, not scanning.
