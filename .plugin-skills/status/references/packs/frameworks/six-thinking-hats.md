# Six Thinking Hats

A way to look at an idea from one viewpoint at a time instead of in an argument: first the facts, then feelings, then the cautions, then the benefits, then new options, then what to decide next. Everyone looks the same way at the same moment, so enthusiasts and sceptics are both heard in full.

## When to use

- The people who decide are split into camps (one keen, one against) and the discussion keeps mixing facts, feelings and objections.
- Alternatives (D) or risks (K) are open, and the user wants a fair hearing of each side before choosing.
- Objections are emotional or unspoken, and naming them would unblock the decision.

## When not to use

- The board expects a view of the company's strengths and weaknesses beside outside opportunities and threats: use `swot`. Choose one of the two for a category.
- The risk is a specific way the rollout could fail: use `pre-mortem`, which this card's caution view can feed.
- The decision-makers already agree and only facts are missing; ask for the facts directly.

## Inputs

- The idea, its outcome and the alternatives already on file.
- The people who decide and their positions, as the user describes them.

## Questions

Ask one viewpoint per question, in this order, in plain words; never name the method. One round at most; when the round holds fewer than six questions, keep facts, cautions and new options.

1. "Chỉ nói về sự thật: hiện mình đã biết chắc những gì về việc này, và còn thiếu thông tin gì?"
2. "Gác lý lẽ sang một bên: mọi người đang cảm thấy thế nào về ý tưởng này, cả hào hứng lẫn lo lắng?"
3. "Nhìn kỹ mặt rủi ro: điều gì có thể làm việc này không đạt, hoặc gây hại?"
4. "Nhìn kỹ mặt lợi: nếu thành công, ai được gì?"
5. "Có cách làm nào khác, hoặc một bản thử nhỏ hơn, mà cả hai bên đều chấp nhận được không?"
6. "Để ra quyết định, cần chốt điều gì trước và ai chốt?"

## Writes to

- Alternatives (D): new options from question 5, and the facts from question 1 that separate the options.
- Cost & Risk (K): the cautions from question 3, each with its likelihood and impact note.
- Assumptions and open questions: the missing facts from question 1, with who can supply them.
- Decisions: what must be settled first and by whom (question 6); feelings from question 2 are recorded in the user's words as concerns, never as facts.
- Diagram brief: none. Show a table with one row per viewpoint and what it found.

## Common misuse

- Labelling a person as one viewpoint ("the finance director is the cautious one"); everyone takes each viewpoint in turn.
- Letting the caution view take over the round; each viewpoint gets its turn.
- Recording feelings as facts, or a benefit as a measured result.
- Inventing figures to settle the debate. Record only figures the user gives, with their source.

## Fashion example (fictional)

The fictional chain "Mây Hồng" considers renting party dresses. The sales director is keen; the finance director objects. Taken one viewpoint at a time, the facts show no data yet on how often a dress can be rented; the caution view names cleaning cost and damage; the benefit view names wedding-season customers lost to rental shops. A new option comes up that both accept: rent only in five stores for one season. That option goes into Alternatives, and the missing rental-frequency figure becomes an open question for the pilot.
