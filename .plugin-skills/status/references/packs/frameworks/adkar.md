# ADKAR

A way to plan how people take up a change, one group at a time. Five steps in order: awareness of why, desire to take part, knowledge of how, ability to do it in practice, and reinforcement so it lasts. The step where people are stuck decides what to do next.

## When to use

- The idea changes how store staff, supervisors or back-office teams work (new tool, new routine, changed KPI).
- Organizational change (L) is Missing or Partial, and the plan needs training and support.
- A past rollout in stores was ignored or worked around.

## When not to use

- No one's work changes (a report only the director reads).
- The change is tiny and reversible, like a label rename.
- As a communication plan alone; it shows where people are stuck, which a notice will not fix.

## Inputs

- The groups whose work changes (store staff, store managers, warehouse, customer care) and how many people.
- What each group does today and what it will do after.
- What they gain or lose: time, authority, targets, commission.
- Past rollouts and why they stalled.

## Questions

Ask per group, starting with the group that changes most.

1. "Nhân viên đã biết vì sao công ty làm việc này chưa, hay họ chỉ nghe là có thêm việc?" (Awareness)
2. "Họ được gì và mất gì: thời gian, thưởng, quyền quyết định?" (Desire)
3. "Họ đã biết làm từng bước chưa, và ai sẽ chỉ họ?" (Knowledge)
4. "Giờ cao điểm họ có thời gian và thiết bị để làm không?" (Ability)
5. "Sau ba tháng, ai kiểm tra và chỉ số nào cho biết họ vẫn đang làm?" (Reinforcement)

If an answer shows a step is weak, record it as the stuck step and plan for that step and the ones after it.

## Writes to

- Cost & Risk and Plan (L): training time, support in the first weeks, changed staff KPIs, named owners.
- Risks: groups likely to resist and what they lose.
- Open questions: groups the user could not describe yet.

## Common misuse

- Skipping desire: training people who do not want the change wastes the training.
- One plan for everyone; store managers and shop-floor staff get stuck at different steps.
- Ending at the go-live day; reinforcement is the step that decides if the change holds.
- Guessing how staff feel. Ask the user, or mark it as an assumption to test in the pilot.

## Fashion example (fictional)

A fictional chain, "Mây Trắng", moves to tablets for size checks on the shop floor. Awareness: staff think it is a way to watch them. Desire: low, because they fear lost commission when stock is sent to another store. Knowledge and ability: fine, the tablet is simple. The stuck step is desire, so the plan adds a clear rule that commission stays with the store that served the shopper, a short talk from store managers in the first week, and a check at week 12 of how often the tablet is used. Training hours are small; the commission rule is the real item to resolve before the pilot.
