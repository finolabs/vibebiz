# 4P / 7P marketing mix

A checklist of the levers a retail offer pulls. The four classic ones are product, price, place and promotion. For a service delivered in stores, add three more: people, process and physical evidence. Use it to test whether an idea fits the market and to see which lever it actually moves.

## When to use

- The idea changes what is sold, how it is priced, where it is sold or how it is promoted (a new collection drop, a bundle, a loyalty offer, pick-up in store).
- The idea is a service experience in stores (fitting help, alterations, styling), so people, process and physical evidence matter.
- The plan needs a market-fit check before cost and return are sized (M).

## When not to use

- The idea is internal and never touches the customer offer (stock reconciliation, internal reporting).
- You are still hunting the root cause of a problem; use the problem-framing questions first.
- As a substitute for the investment case: the mix says what the offer is, not whether it pays back.

## Inputs

- The proposed offer in one sentence and who it is for.
- The current mix: range, price points, channels and stores, promotion calendar.
- Which lever the idea is meant to move, and which levers it will disturb by accident.
- For store services: how many staff touch the experience and what the shopper sees and feels.

## Questions

Ask only the levers the idea touches; skip the rest and note them as unchanged.

1. "Ý tưởng này thay đổi sản phẩm nào: mẫu mới, size, chất liệu, hay chỉ cách bán?"
2. "Giá thay đổi thế nào, và có làm hàng đang bán ở giá cũ khó bán hơn không?"
3. "Khách mua ở đâu: cửa hàng, web, sàn thương mại điện tử? Kênh nào sẽ được ưu tiên?"
4. "Ai sẽ biết về điều này và bằng cách nào: tin nhắn, bảng hiệu, nhân viên nói trực tiếp?"
5. "Nhân viên nào phải làm khác đi, quy trình nào đổi, và khách sẽ nhìn thấy gì khác trong cửa hàng?"

## Writes to

- Scope and behavior (E): User Stories and Functional Requirements for the levers that change.
- Cost & Risk and Plan (M): price and promotion assumptions, marked "not tech-reviewed" when they carry estimates.
- Alternatives: levers that were considered and left unchanged, with the reason.

## Common misuse

- Filling all seven boxes with generic text; an unchanged lever gets one line, not a paragraph.
- Treating the mix as a strategy; it is a checklist, and it does not rank the levers.
- Setting a price or promotion uplift the user never stated; record a gap instead.
- Forgetting that moving one lever shifts another (a deeper discount hits margin and brand).

## Fashion example (fictional)

The fictional brand "Gió Mùa" plans a "try at home, pay for what you keep" offer for its online store. Product: no change in range. Price: same price, free return shipping. Place: online only; stores take returns. Promotion: sent to existing members first. People: store staff will handle returns, so their time rises. Process: a return inspection step is added. Physical evidence: a branded bag and a clear return slip. The check shows the real cost sits in people and process, not in price, so the plan sizes return handling hours first and labels the cost "not tech-reviewed".
