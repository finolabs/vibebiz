# Build, buy or partner with return and payback

Three ways to get a capability: make it, purchase a ready product, or work with a partner. Put each next to the others, with cost, return and payback in three scenarios, so the board decides on numbers and not on enthusiasm.

## When to use

- The alternatives (D) name more than one route to the same outcome, or the user assumes "we will build it" without comparing.
- The plan needs an investment case (M): what it costs, what it returns, when it pays back.
- Many retail tools already exist as products (chatbots, size advice, forecasting), so buying is a real option.

## When not to use

- The outcome and baseline are not set (C is not Clear): the return has nothing to stand on.
- Only one route is legal, required or already decided; record the reason and skip the comparison.
- The user wants a precise financial model. This card gives a decision-grade comparison, not an audit.

## Inputs

- The outcome and its baseline and target, from the outcome lens.
- For each route: one-time cost, yearly running cost, time to first use, the team needed.
- The return driver: extra revenue, saved hours, fewer returns, with its source and period.
- The approval ceiling from the ground rules, when recorded.

## Questions

Never invent figures. When the user does not know, record a range and a gap.

1. "Bạn đã biết có sản phẩm nào có sẵn trên thị trường làm được việc này chưa, hay đối tác nào có thể làm cùng?"
2. "Mỗi hướng ước tính tốn bao nhiêu lúc đầu và bao nhiêu mỗi năm? Bạn cho một khoảng thấp, vừa, cao."
3. "Khi chạy được, khoản thu thêm hoặc tiết kiệm được lớn nhất đến từ đâu, và con số hiện tại là bao nhiêu?"
4. "Hướng nào cần đội kỹ thuật của công ty, và công ty đã đủ người chưa?"
5. "Nếu phải chờ lâu hơn dự kiến một quý, hướng nào vẫn đáng làm?"

## Writes to

- Alternatives (D): the three routes beside "do nothing" and "fix the process".
- Cost & Risk and Plan (M): cost, return and payback in low, base and high.
- Diagram brief: a bar chart of return in the three scenarios, with source and period.

Every cost and every return figure carries the label "not tech-reviewed" until the tech team reviews it, and each is shown as low, base and high. Payback is the one-time cost divided by the yearly net return, stated in months, and left blank when the return is unknown.

## Common misuse

- Showing a single number; always low, base and high.
- Counting only the build cost and leaving out running cost, training and the time of store staff.
- Treating "buy" as free of effort; it still needs setup, data and change work.
- Presenting estimates as reviewed. They stay "not tech-reviewed".
- Choosing the route first and fitting the numbers after.

## Fashion example (fictional)

The fictional chain "Thảo Nguyên" wants size and fit advice online to cut size-related returns. Build: highest upfront cost, slow start. Buy: a ready size-advice product, monthly fee, quick pilot. Partner: a marketplace tool that only works on its own site. Illustrative ranges, all "not tech-reviewed": buying costs 450 / 300 / 200 million VND in year one in the low / base / high cases, with a yearly return from fewer returns of 150 / 400 / 700 million VND. Payback is then about 36 / 9 / 3.4 months, so the board sees the downside case clearly and the pilot decision is framed around it.
